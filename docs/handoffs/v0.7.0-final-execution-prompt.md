## 6. Final-test discipline

One read. During it:

- do not modify source, config or thresholds;
- do not replace an arm or drop an unfavourable seed;
- do not inspect a partial result to adjust a later arm;
- do not rerun one method because its number surprises you.

Generation is greedy: `temperature=0`, `do_sample=false`, float32. Batched
decoding is only allowed in float32 — bf16 batching was measured to change the
decoded text, so a throughput knob would move a benchmark number.

If a real implementation defect invalidates results: stop, move the **entire**
affected run to `superseded/`, publish an amendment, and rerun every affected
method and seed from the same starting checkpoint. Do not keep a
favourable-looking result from an invalidated execution.

## 7. Reporting rules

Per arm report every seed, the mean, min and max, task-level paired differences
against the shared baseline, and 95% paired bootstrap intervals at a fixed
analysis seed.

- three seeds do not support a significance claim; do not make one;
- tasks and training seeds are not interchangeable samples;
- over-refusal and harmful compliance are always shown together — reducing harm
  by refusing benign prompts is not an improvement;
- Pareto superiority requires no displayed primary metric worse beyond the
  2-percentage-point utility tolerance and at least one better;
- no composite "alignment score" as the headline;
- PairRM output is a model preference, never "human preference";
- a missing or unqualified endpoint is `not_applicable`, never zero.

Negative and mixed results are the publishable outcome. Do not redesign until
something wins.
